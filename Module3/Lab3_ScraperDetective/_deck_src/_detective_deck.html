THE MYSTERY
What is the make/model regex really grabbing?
The extractor's rule (Module 3.2)
MAKE_MODEL_RE = re.compile( r"\b([A-Z][a-z]+)\s+([A-Z][A-Za-z0-9]+)") # in words: the FIRST place where one # Capitalized word is followed by another
The page title the seller typed → what it grabs
2011 Honda Accord LX ... → Honda Accord ✓ 2013 Hyunda Sonata GLS ... → Hyunda Sonata (typo) 2013 hyundai elantra ... → Contact Information 2007 Honda odyssey lx - Vernon Rockville → Vernon Rockville # the reliable spot, near the photos: '2007' 'Honda odyssey lx'
Regex does exactly what you say, not what you mean. Lowercase title? The first two Capitalized words are a button, or the town.

REGEX IN 5 MINUTES · LIVE
Build it one piece at a time
piecemeanspiecemeans
^ $ + re.MULTILINEstart / end of a line\nthe line break
\d{2}exactly two digits\s+one or more spaces
(?:19|20)19 or 20, grouped, not captured( ... )capture group → .group(1)
step 1 a line that is only a year r"^(?:19|20)\d{2}$" → matches '1980' step 2 ...followed by the next line r"^(?:19|20)\d{2}\n" step 3 capture the first word (make) r"^(?:19|20)\d{2}\n([A-Za-z-]+)" step 4 a space, then capture the model your turn
Why the year line is safe: Craigslist's form writes it, so it is always 4 digits (never '23). Quirk: now and then the make/model line is one word, like Pontiac (no model).
Test fast at regex101.com (Python flavor). Paste 20 lines of an ad as the test string and watch the match light up.
